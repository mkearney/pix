# Prior art
Claim type: reversal
Naive prediction: If tech giants are involved in a quarter of new cables, they account for about a quarter of new cable laid.
Candidates considered:
- "By length, most new undersea cable has a Big Tech owner" (reversal: count vs kilometres, TeleGeography map data). Chosen: own measure, and it explains why published figures disagree.
- "Most new undersea cable km is now part-owned by Big Tech" (crossover, same data). Folded into the chosen one: TeleGeography already says content providers are the biggest investors in new cables, so the crossover alone re-reports their point.
- "Only seven of 36 big power systems got dirtier since 2010" (outlier, Ember via OWID). Lost: the standout, Japan, is the well-known Fukushima story.
- "Satellites now fall back to Earth three times a day" (turning-point, CelesTrak SATCAT). Lost: ESA's 2026 report charts the re-entry rate, and Starlink deorbits are widely covered.
- "This year's cables beat the dot-com peak" (crossover, same map data). Lost: the map drops retired cables, so the old years are undercounted and the comparison is rigged.

What this adds: A reproducible kilometre measure from the public map, set against the count. A quarter of new cables but over half the length, because the median big-tech cable is 7.4 times longer. That gap is why the published shares disagree, and I didn't find it stated anywhere.
