# Prior art
Claim type: turning-point
Naive prediction: Satellites keep getting smaller, since everyone has heard of CubeSats and cheap small launches, and the typical satellite today is a few kilograms.
Candidates considered:
- "Satellites stopped shrinking in 2017" (turning-point, GCAT median mass by launch year). Chosen: own computation from the full catalog, and the rise holds without Starlink.
- "Working seniors now outnumber teens in the US labour force" (crossover, BLS via FRED). Lost: reported in 2010 (San Francisco Chronicle, Fortune) and repeated many times since.
- "2025 put more satellite mass in orbit than any Cold War year" (crossover, GCAT). Lost: BryceTech publishes yearly upmass totals and Ars Technica charts them.
- "The Philippines' power got dirtier faster than any big grid" (outlier, Ember via OWID). Lost: Japan and Iraq also got dirtier, so it isn't a clean outlier.
- "Satellites now re-enter three times a day" (turning-point, CelesTrak). Lost: ESA's 2026 report charts re-entries.

Closest takes:
- Michael Swartwout, "The Sixth Age of Small Satellites", https://sites.google.com/a/slu.edu/swartwout/cubesat-database/six-ages-of-small-satellites: plots mean and median spacecraft mass by year (Seradata data) but doesn't name the low point or the size of the rebound.
- Starburst, citing BryceTech, https://starburst.aero/news/launch-after-launch/: outside Starlink and OneWeb, satellites over 200 kg rose 119% from 2020 to 2024 while those under 10 kg fell 45%.
- BryceTech Smallsats by the Numbers 2025, https://brycetech.com/reports/report-documents/smallsats-2025/BryceTech_Smallsats-by-the-Numbers-2025.pdf: record average smallsat mass in 2024.
What this adds: The turning point dated and sized from the full open catalog through November 2026: the median fell from 1,450 kg to 5 kg in 2017 and is now 575 kg, 122 times higher. The rise survives removing Starlink (5 kg to 267 kg).
