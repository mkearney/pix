# Prior art
Claim type: gap
Naive prediction: Coffee and cocoa are both tropical tree crops sold into the same kind of market, so their output growth since the late 1980s should split between land and yield in roughly the same way.
Candidates considered:
- "Cocoa doubled its harvest by doubling its land" (gap, FAOSTAT area x yield decomposition, coffee as the comparison). Chosen: own decomposition, a comparison no one seems to have charted.
- "Working satellites now outnumber debris in orbit" (crossover, CelesTrak SATCAT). Lost: true in the public US catalog but not in ESA's fuller one, so the crossover depends on which catalog you pick.
- "Cocoa is the one crop the yield boom skipped" (outlier, OWID key crop yields). Lost: not true. Cassava's yield grew less (1.33x since 1961 against cocoa's 1.68x).
- "World cocoa yields stopped rising in 1988" (turning-point, OWID). Lost: the flat line is visible in OWID's own cocoa yield chart, and cassava is just as flat.
- "Since 1988 cocoa farms spread over an area the size of Togo" (equivalence). Lost: the archive used a country equivalence on 2026-11-09.

Closest takes:
- ICRAF/World Agroforestry brief, https://apps.worldagroforestry.org/downloads/Publications/PDFS/BC22010.pdf: area expansion, not yield, explains most production growth for soy, oil palm, cocoa and coffee taken together.
- World Coffee Research (2025), https://worldcoffeeresearch.org/download/fb982df0-4253-408f-b6e8-8ec8d97ded80: coffee output rose from 93 to 167 million bags since 1990-91, mostly from efficiency gains in Brazil and Vietnam.
- Our World in Data, cocoa bean yields, https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/cocoa-bean-yields: shows the flat world yield but doesn't set it against land or other crops.
What this adds: The two crops decomposed side by side with the same method and window (88% of cocoa's growth from land, 18% for coffee), plus the same measure for the 21 biggest crops by area. Brazil growing more coffee on a million fewer hectares, against Côte d'Ivoire's 2.8 million new hectares of cocoa at flat yields, is the contrast I didn't find anywhere.
