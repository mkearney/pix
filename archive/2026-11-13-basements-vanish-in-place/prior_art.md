# Prior art
Claim type: mechanism
Naive prediction: The basement is disappearing because new building moved to the South and West, where houses sit on slabs, so the regional mix explains most of the fall.
Candidates considered:
- "The move South explains only a third of lost basements" (mechanism, Census SOC microdata). Chosen: the trend is reported every year, but nobody has split it into geography and within-region change, and the answer runs against the obvious story.
- "Slabs overtook basements in the Great Lakes states" (turning-point, same data). Lost: NAHB's October 2026 post makes exactly this point for East North Central.
- "Starlink is most of what falls out of orbit by count, not by mass" (reversal, McDowell GCAT). Lost: Jonathan McDowell tracks re-entries and mass publicly, and the mass fields are patchy.
- "Unicode gives dead scripts more room than living ones" (outlier, Unicode Scripts.txt and speaker counts). Lost: mostly reflects script type (logographic vs alphabetic), close to trivia.
- "Big Tech now co-owns most new undersea cable" (turning-point, TeleGeography). Lost: TeleGeography and the FT have covered content providers' share of new cables.

What this adds: A shift-share decomposition across nine divisions and a counterfactual series. Geography explains 33% of the fall since 2000; northern builders dropping basements explain 36% on their own. No source I found quantifies the split.
