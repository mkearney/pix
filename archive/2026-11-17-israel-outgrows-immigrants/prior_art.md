# Prior art
Claim type: outlier
Naive prediction: Rich countries all became more foreign-born since 1990, and a classic immigrant nation like Israel stayed near the top of the list.
Candidates considered:
- "Israel is outgrowing its immigrants" (outlier, UN migrant stock via OWID, own native-born split). Chosen: the share falls while the count rises, which no other rich country does at anything like that size.
- "The Baltics are the rich world's shrinking immigrant nations" (outlier, same data). Folded in as the contrast: Pew has already noted Latvia's and Estonia's falling shares.
- "China passed the EU in electricity use per person" (crossover, Ember via OWID). Lost without searching: the rise-of-China theme, and I'd expect Ember to have covered it.
- "Working seniors outnumber teens in the US labour force" (crossover, BLS). Lost: reported since 2010.
- "Chicken passed pork as the world's top meat" (crossover, FAOSTAT). Lost without searching: OWID's meat production chart would show it at a glance.

Closest takes:
- OECD International Migration Outlook 2013, Israel chapter, https://www.oecd-ilibrary.org/social-issues-migration-health/international-migration-outlook-2013/israel_migr_outlook-2013-23-en: the foreign-born are a quarter of residents "and this proportion is declining".
- Pew Research (2019), https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2019/01/30/immigrant-share-in-u-s-nears-record-high-but-remains-below-that-of-many-other-countries/: immigrant shares dropping in Latvia and Estonia as emigration exceeds immigration and immigrants age.
- Our World in Data, migrant stock share, https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/migrant-stock-share: the underlying series, no comparison of decliners.
What this adds: Israel set against all 51 rich countries, and the split into immigrants and native-born that shows two opposite routes to the same fall: Israel's immigrants rose 29% while its native-born grew 2.5x; Latvia's immigrants fell by two-thirds.
