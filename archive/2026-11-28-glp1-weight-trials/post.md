# Obesity drug users lost 15% to 21% in trials

![Dumbbell chart of mean weight change by the end of each trial: STEP 1, semaglutide minus 14.9% against minus 2.4% on placebo; STEP 3, minus 16.0% against minus 5.7%; SURMOUNT-1, tirzepatide minus 15.0%, 19.5% and 20.9% at 5, 10 and 15 mg against minus 3.1%. Below, in STEP 4, people who stayed on semaglutide lost a further 7.9% while those switched to placebo gained back 6.9% of their week-20 weight.](chart.png)

In large randomised trials, people given weekly semaglutide or tirzepatide lost far more weight than those on placebo. Over 68 weeks, people on semaglutide lost 14.9% of their weight against 2.4% on placebo. Over 72 weeks, people on tirzepatide lost 15.0% to 20.9%, depending on dose, against 3.1%. Weight came back when the drug stopped: in a withdrawal trial, people switched to placebo gained back 6.9% of their body weight, while those who stayed on semaglutide lost a further 7.9%.

**Source:** Wilding JPH, Batterham RL, Calanna S, et al. Once-weekly semaglutide in adults with overweight or obesity. *N Engl J Med* 384, 989–1002 (2021). [doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2032183](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183); Jastreboff AM, Aronne LJ, Ahmad NN, et al. Tirzepatide once weekly for the treatment of obesity. *N Engl J Med* 387, 205–216 (2022). [doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2206038](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2206038)<br>
**Evidence:** Double-blind randomised trials of 611 to 2,539 adults with obesity (BMI 30 or more) or overweight with a related condition, without diabetes, all funded by the manufacturers. Confirmed by STEP 3 and by meta-analyses of 56 trials (*Nature Medicine*, 2025) and of semaglutide trials (*American Journal of Cardiology*, 2024). Numbers transcribed from the trial abstracts.
