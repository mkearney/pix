# Evidence

Finding: In adults with obesity and without diabetes, weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg and tirzepatide cause large weight loss compared with placebo over 68–72 weeks, and weight returns when the drug is stopped.

Type: effect.

Source: Wilding JPH, Batterham RL, Calanna S, et al.; STEP 1 Study Group. Once-weekly semaglutide in adults with overweight or obesity. N Engl J Med 384(11), 989–1002 (2021). doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2032183. PubMed 33567185. And Jastreboff AM, Aronne LJ, Ahmad NN, et al.; SURMOUNT-1 Investigators. Tirzepatide once weekly for the treatment of obesity. N Engl J Med 387(3), 205–216 (2022). doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2206038. PubMed 35658024. No individual data released.

Design: Double-blind, placebo-controlled randomised trials, all arms with lifestyle intervention. STEP 1: 1,961 adults (BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with a weight-related condition, no diabetes), 2:1 semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo, 68 weeks. SURMOUNT-1: 2,539 adults, 1:1:1:1 tirzepatide 5, 10 or 15 mg or placebo, 72 weeks. STEP 3: 611 adults, 2:1, with intensive behavioural therapy and an initial low-calorie diet, 68 weeks. STEP 4: 803 adults who completed a 20-week semaglutide run-in, randomised to continue or switch to placebo for 48 weeks. Primary analyses by treatment policy (regardless of discontinuation).

Key numbers:
- STEP 1: −14.9% vs −2.4%; difference −12.4 points (95% CI −13.4 to −11.5). Abstract. Transcribed and asserted.
- SURMOUNT-1: −15.0% (−15.9 to −14.2), −19.5% (−20.4 to −18.5), −20.9% (−21.8 to −19.9) at 5, 10, 15 mg vs −3.1% (−4.3 to −1.9). Abstract. Transcribed and asserted.
- STEP 3: −16.0% vs −5.7%; difference −10.3 (−12.0 to −8.6). Abstract. Chart only.
- STEP 4: weeks 20–68, −7.9% continued vs +6.9% switched to placebo (percent of week-20 weight; treatment-policy estimand in all 803 randomised 2:1, 535 vs 268); difference −14.8 (−16.0 to −13.5); run-in loss 10.6%. Abstract and full text (PMC7988425). Those switched regained part, not all, of the run-in loss within 48 weeks.
- "15% to 21%" in the headline: rounded range of the drug-arm means (14.9% to 20.9%), described as weight people lost, not as the drug's effect; placebo-adjusted differences are 10.3 to 17.8 points (SURMOUNT-1 differences derived here, as the abstract gives no CI for them).

Corroboration:
- Wadden TA, Bailey TS, Billings LK, et al. Effect of subcutaneous semaglutide vs placebo as an adjunct to intensive behavioral therapy on body weight in adults with overweight or obesity: the STEP 3 randomized clinical trial. JAMA 325(14), 1403–1413 (2021). doi:10.1001/jama.2021.1831. −16.0% vs −5.7%.
- A systematic review and meta-analysis of the efficacy and safety of pharmacological treatments for obesity in adults. Nat Med 31, 3317–3329 (2025). doi:10.1038/s41591-025-03978-z. 56 trials, 60,307 patients; total body weight loss more than 10% versus placebo for semaglutide and tirzepatide.
- Long-term efficacy and safety of once-weekly semaglutide for weight loss in patients without diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Am J Cardiol 222, 121–130 (2024). doi:10.1016/j.amjcard.2024.04.041. Weighted mean difference −12.1% (95% CI −13.5 to −10.7) versus placebo at ≥68 weeks.
- Rubino D, et al. STEP 4. JAMA 325(14), 1414–1425 (2021). doi:10.1001/jama.2021.3224 (withdrawal).
- Wilding JPH, et al. Weight regain and cardiometabolic effects after withdrawal of semaglutide: the STEP 1 trial extension. Diabetes Obes Metab 24, 1553–1564 (2022). doi:10.1111/dom.14725. About two-thirds of lost weight regained within a year of stopping.
- Aronne LJ, et al. SURMOUNT-4. JAMA 331(1), 38–48 (2024). doi:10.1001/jama.2023.24945. Weeks 36–88: −5.5% continuing tirzepatide vs +14.0% switched to placebo.
- People with type 2 diabetes lose less: STEP 2 (Davies et al., Lancet 2021, doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(21)00213-0), −9.6% vs −3.4%; SURMOUNT-2 (Garvey et al., Lancet 2023, doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(23)01200-X), −12.8% and −14.7% vs −3.2%.

Wording check: Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, replicated, so causal claims are supported. The headline and summary describe weight people lost on each arm rather than crediting the drug with the whole loss, since placebo groups also lost 2.4% to 5.7%. Percentages are means from the start of each trial; individuals vary. The trials are separate, not head-to-head.

Limits: All trials were funded by the manufacturers (Novo Nordisk, Eli Lilly). The results are consistent across meta-analyses, which pool the same trials; most Nat Med 2025 authors declare industry fees, while the Am J Cardiol 2024 authors declare none. Participants were mostly women and excluded people with diabetes, who lose less weight on these drugs. Weight returns after stopping (STEP 4; the STEP 1 extension and SURMOUNT-4 show the same), and part of the weight lost is lean mass. Gastrointestinal side effects were common, mostly mild to moderate, and led some to stop.

Fact-check: An independent subagent confirmed every number against the PubMed abstracts and the STEP 4 full text (estimands, 2:1 randomisation, 10.6% run-in loss, 79–81% women), confirmed both meta-analyses, the STEP 1 extension, SURMOUNT-4 and the smaller losses in diabetes (STEP 2, SURMOUNT-2), and found no retraction, correction or dispute of efficacy. Verdict: pass with fixes.
