# Prior art
Claim type: reversal
Naive prediction: Countries with long hours per worker also work the most per adult, so Italy, Greece and Chile stay well above the Netherlands and the Nordics.
Candidates considered:
- "Italy drops 19 places when every adult counts" (reversal, OECD hours x employment). Chosen: own derived measure, current data, a full re-ranking nobody publishes as a chart.
- "The Dutch outwork the Italians" (same data). Lost: Statistics Netherlands made this exact point in 2017.
- "Greeks work the longest hours in Europe and among the fewest per adult" (same data). Lost: not true. Greece falls 16 places but stays above Germany.
- "New homes in the US stopped getting bigger in 2015" (turning-point, Census). Lost: well covered by housing press and NAHB.
- "Container ships stopped growing at 24,000 TEU" (turning-point, ship lists). Lost: shipping press covers "peak ship size", and the data is a hand-built Wikipedia list.

Closest takes:
- Statistics Netherlands via DutchNews (2017), https://www.dutchnews.nl/news/2017/08/lazy-or-efficient-dutch-work-the-least-of-all-europeans-says-cbs: per working-age person the Dutch work 20 hours a week, and only Spain, Croatia, Greece and Italy work less (EU, 2016 data).
- Gethin and Saez, "Global Working Hours" (NBER w34217, 2025), https://www.nber.org/papers/w34217: hours per adult for 160 countries, framed around the bell-shaped link with income.
- Our World in Data, "Working hours", https://ourworldindata.org/working-hours: notes that hours per person = hours per worker x employment rate, but charts hours per worker.
What this adds: The full OECD ranking on both measures for 2024, side by side, showing who moves and by how much (10 of 40 countries move 10 or more places). The Dutch-Italian flip is known; the size of Italy's fall and Iceland's and Japan's rises are not charted anywhere I found.
